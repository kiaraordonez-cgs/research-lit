# Protecting the poor with a carbon tax and equal per capita dividend

> [!summary] One-sentence summary
> A flat per-capita dividend funded by carbon-tax revenue can flip the policy from regressive to net-progressive, raising welfare for the poorest even as the tax itself raises energy prices.

## Why I read it / how it connects
- Core reference for the distributional-design question in my work
- Direct companion to [[Climate action with revenue recycling]] — both argue recycling design dominates the regressivity worry
- Counterpoint to the naive "carbon taxes hurt the poor" framing

## Key points
- Revenue recycling mechanism, not the tax rate, determines who wins and loses
- Equal per-capita dividend is progressive because the poor consume less carbon in absolute terms
- Result holds across a range of regions and income distributions

## Methods / model
- Model(s): integrated welfare model with regional income distributions
- Regional/sector resolution: multi-region, household income deciles
- Key assumptions worth remembering: dividend returned lump-sum and equal per capita; abstracts from political-economy of implementation

## Open questions / critiques
- Assumes clean lump-sum return; real-world recycling is messier
- How sensitive is the progressivity result to administrative leakage?
